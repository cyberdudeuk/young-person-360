Every young person who is NEET was once held by something

NEET counts young people aged 16 to 24 at a point in time. This portal looks earlier and wider: the whole young person, the bodies that hold a piece of them, and the points where those pieces stop connecting.

BEETBeing in Education, Employment or Training. The default engaged state: universal, not individually tracked once things go well.
CHEETChild Held in Early years, Education or Training. Held by the state or by someone other than their parents: care, guardianship, treatment, custody of a parent, licensed work.
NEETNot in Education, Employment or Training, measured at 16 to 24. A status, never a person. Covered in depth by the separate "Earning, learning and training" portal.

The case against a 360 view, stated first

A complete view of a child could become surveillance. Families have a right to private life, most children need no record of their home, online or community life, and linking data creates new risks if it is lost or misused. Reusing an identifier built for health care across other services sharpens these risks.

How this portal answers it: it maps the system's sight, not any child. Grey domains are "not recorded, as expected" and are not shown as failures. Gaps are only flagged where a public body already holds responsibility for the child (care, licensing, education, justice) but cannot see what another body knows.

Identifiers

A whole view needs a shared key. Today a young person passes through several identifiers, each owned by a different body, and none spans the whole of childhood and early adulthood.

What the 2026 Act changes, and what it does not

The Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 creates a duty to share information for safeguarding and welfare, and a power to specify a consistent identifier that designated persons must include. It is scoped to safeguarding and welfare purposes and to children, so on its own it does not carry a young person across 18, and the regulations that name the identifier had not been found as published at the evidence date.
